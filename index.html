About

As a kid, I dreamed about the life I would build — the job I’d have, the person I’d become, even the car I’d drive. Like many people, I thought life would follow the plan I made. But real growth doesn’t come from sticking to the plan — it comes from facing setbacks and learning to adapt.

I started my academic journey studying Biology at Burman University in Lacombe, Alberta (don't worry, I had to look it up too), where I attended on a basketball scholarship. At the time, I didn’t know exactly what I wanted to do — Biology felt like a safe choice because I had always done well in science. But as the years passed, I realized something important: I wasn’t passionate about the path I was on.

After graduating, I found myself at a crossroads. I spent time reflecting on what truly excited me, and the answer kept pointing back to technology. Growing up, I was always curious about computers — whether it was gaming, browsing the web, or just wondering how everything worked behind the scenes. Even though I had never written a single line of code, I trusted that curiosity and took a leap of faith. I enrolled at York University's Lassonde School of Engineering to study Computer Science.

That decision changed everything. It taught me that failure isn’t something to fear — it’s a necessary part of growth. Every setback, every challenge, every misstep is a chance to learn and get better.

Today, as I continue my journey in software engineering, I hold onto one core belief: fail forward. Growth comes from stepping outside of your comfort zone, embracing the unknown, and using every experience — good or bad — to build something stronger. I’m excited to keep learning, building, and pushing myself to become the best engineer I can be.
